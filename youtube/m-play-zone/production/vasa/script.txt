In sixteen twenty-eight, Sweden built the mightiest warship in the world. It sank before it even left the harbor.
The king wanted a monster. Sixty-four bronze cannons. Hundreds of golden carvings. Two full decks of guns.
Nobody argued. You don't argue with a king. So they kept stacking weight on top of a ship that was already too narrow.
Before launch, they ran a test. Thirty sailors ran from one side of the deck to the other. After three runs, the ship was rocking so hard, the admiral stopped the test.
And then he made a bold decision. He let it sail anyway.
On launch day, thousands of people lined the shore. The cannons fired a salute. The sails opened.
A light breeze hit. The ship leaned, and came back. Then a second gust pushed it over, and water poured in through the open gun ports.
Twenty minutes. Thirteen hundred meters. That was the whole voyage.
For three hundred and thirty-three years, it sat at the bottom. But the cold, dark water kept it almost perfect.
Today, it's a museum, with more than a million visitors a year.
The ship that never made it out of port... became Sweden's most popular trip.
