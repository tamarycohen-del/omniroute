ACCURACY NOTES
- Facts used (well documented): nine hikers, mostly Ural Polytechnic Institute students/graduates, led by Igor Dyatlov (23);
  camped on the slope of Kholat Syakhl on the night of 1-2 February 1959; tent found 26 February, cut open from the inside,
  most footwear left behind; tracks in socks/one shoe/bare feet led down toward the forest; the first were found by a cedar
  with the remains of a fire; the last four were found in May in a ravine; some had severe injuries; some clothing was
  reported to show radioactive contamination; the Soviet case was closed citing "a compelling natural force";
  in 2020 Russian prosecutors concluded a snow (slab) avalanche plus hypothermia.
- Because there IS an official explanation (avalanche, 2020) the video says "still debated", not "unsolved".
- No bodies or injuries are drawn; the group "silhouettes" are anonymous; no culprit or theory is presented as fact.
- "Kholat Syakhl" is usually translated "Dead Mountain" (Mansi); some translate it differently.
- Illustrated reconstruction, no real photos.
