In this town, everyone has a clone that does the exact opposite of everything they do.

Yesterday, I looked my clone right in the eyes and said, "I hope you NEVER become famous."

This morning, I opened my front door... and the entire town was standing outside my house. And they weren't there for me.

Okay, let me back up. My name is Alex. Blue cap, yellow star. You can't miss me.

In my town, the day you turn ten, you wake up... and there's someone standing next to your bed. Someone who looks exactly like you. Same face. Same clothes. Same everything. Your Opposite.

When I got mine, I said, "Hi! I'm Alex!" It didn't say a word. It just turned around... and walked straight into my closet. I figured we'd become best friends eventually. Spoiler: we did not.

And the rules are simple. If you smile, your Opposite frowns.

If you walk, it runs.

If you sit down, it stands up.

If you wave hello, it turns around and walks away. Rude, I know.

At first, people thought it was creepy. But this is a small town, and small towns get bored. So pretty soon, everyone figured out how to have fun with their Opposites.

Take Lazy Larry. Larry hasn't gotten off his couch in three years. Which means his Opposite hasn't stopped working in three years.

It mows the lawn. It washes the car. It paints the fence. Larry has the cleanest house on the entire street, and he's never lifted a finger.

Then there's Mayor Gold. Every time the mayor gives a long, boring speech, his Opposite stands right behind him... yawning. Huge yawns. The whole crowd cracks up. Honestly, it's the only reason anyone shows up.

And then there's the biggest event of the year. The Opposite Race. You don't race. Your Opposite races for you. So the slower you walk, the faster your clone runs.

Last year, a grandma crawled across the finish line like a snail, and her Opposite broke the town record. She got a trophy bigger than her head.

At school, it got even better. Our teacher, Mrs. Bloom, would sit perfectly still at her desk with a cup of coffee... ...while her Opposite zoomed around the classroom, handing out tests, cleaning the board, and watering the plants. Best teacher ever. Technically.

Everybody loved their Opposite. Everybody except me.

My Opposite's name is Xela. That's Alex, backwards. I thought it was clever. Xela did not.

See, I'm kind of different. Most people don't talk to their Opposites. They just use them, like a tool. But I always talked to mine. "Good morning, Xela." "Goodnight, Xela." I even told it about my day. It felt rude not to.

And Xela? Xela seemed to hate me.

When I said, "Please don't embarrass me today," it danced in the middle of class.

When I said, "Stay right here," it wandered all the way across town.

When I said, "Don't eat my last cookie"... gone. Crumbs everywhere.

My best friend Mia thought it was hilarious. Mia's Opposite was the sweetest clone in town. Every time Mia was grumpy, it gave her a hug and brought her flowers. Meanwhile, mine was eating my snacks.

"Maybe it's just broken," Mia said. Maybe. But it didn't feel broken. It felt... on purpose.

And then came the day of the Opposite Race. This was my year. I'd practiced walking slow for weeks.

The whole town showed up. Flags. Snacks. Mia walked like a sleepy turtle, and her Opposite flew around the track like a superhero. She won second place last year. This year, I was taking first.

Before the race, I knelt down next to Xela and said, "Please. Just this once. Help me win."

The whistle blew. I took the slowest step of my life. Every other clone took off like a rocket.

And Xela? Xela sat down. Right in the middle of the track. It crossed its legs, looked up at the sky... and just sat there.

Everyone laughed. The whole school. The mayor. Even Mia. I came in last. Dead last. Behind the grandma.

That night, I was so angry I couldn't sleep. Xela was standing in the corner of my room like always, staring at me.

So I walked right up to it and said the meanest thing I could think of. "You know what? I hope you NEVER become famous. I hope nobody ever even notices you."

Xela didn't move. And then, for the first time in my whole life... my Opposite smiled at me. I wasn't smiling.

The next morning, I heard noise outside. Cheering. Cameras clicking.

I opened the front door, and the entire town was on my lawn. The mayor. A news van. Kids with posters. And in the middle of all of it... Xela.

Overnight, my Opposite had become the most famous person in town.

It rescued a cat from a tree, right in front of a news camera.

It juggled six oranges at the market.

The mayor gave it a gold medal and called it "the pride of our town."

It helped Larry's Opposite paint an entire house in ten minutes. It carried groceries for old ladies. It even fixed the clock on the town hall, which had been broken since before I was born.

And the weirdest part? It wasn't doing the opposite of me anymore. I frowned... it didn't smile. I walked... it didn't run. It just did whatever it wanted.

People started calling it "the better Alex." Kids I'd known my whole life walked right past me to take a selfie with my clone. By the end of the week, I was just the kid who lived in its house.

Even my mom set an extra plate for it at dinner. A bigger plate. With extra fries. I sat there eating my peas while my own clone signed autographs at our kitchen table.

Then it got stranger. Other Opposites started acting weird too. Everywhere Xela went, a line of clones followed right behind it, like it was their leader.

People got scared. What if the Opposites were done being opposite? What if they were planning something?

Mia grabbed my arm. "This started with your clone, Alex. You have to figure this out."

First, I just asked it. "Xela, why are you doing this?" It looked at me, tilted its head... and shrugged. Super helpful.

So we did a test. I walked right up to Xela, pointed at an apple, and said, "Xela. Do NOT pick up that apple." Xela picked up the apple.

Mia's eyes went wide. "Say something else." "Don't jump." It jumped. "Don't wave at Mia." It waved at Mia.

And that's when it hit me. Opposites don't just do the opposite of what you do. They do the opposite of what you SAY. Nobody had ever noticed, because nobody ever talked to their clone. Nobody... except me.

"Don't embarrass me." It danced. "Stay right here." It left. "Help me win." It sat down. And "I hope nobody ever notices you"? It made sure everybody noticed.

Xela never hated me. It was listening to me. The whole time. Every single word.

And all those clones following it around? Their owners kept yelling the same thing. "Don't go near that clone!"

I should've kept that a secret. I really should have. Because a reporter from the news van heard everything. Ten minutes later, it was on every TV in town.

And everybody started talking to their clones. All at once. Larry yelled, "Don't do the dishes!" and his Opposite did the dishes. Okay. That one worked.

But then a kid yelled, "Don't break the fountain!" And... you can guess what happened.

Someone screamed "STOP!" and their clone ran faster. The mayor grabbed a microphone and shouted, "Everyone, DON'T PANIC!"

And every Opposite in town started panicking. Running in circles. Knocking over fruit stands. Jumping on cars. Total chaos.

A lady yelled, "Don't climb the clock tower!" Three seconds later, there were twelve clones on top of the clock tower. A man yelled, "Don't touch my car!" And his Opposite started washing it... with a garden hose... from the inside.

I climbed onto the stage, grabbed the mayor's microphone, and took the biggest breath of my life.

"Everybody! Tell your Opposites to KEEP GOING! Tell them to go FASTER! Tell them to break EVERYTHING!"

People looked at me like I was crazy. But one by one, they started yelling it. "Keep going!" "Faster!" "Break everything!"

And one by one... every Opposite in town stopped. They put down the fruit. They climbed off the cars. They stood perfectly still.

And the first one to stop... was Xela. It looked across the square at me, and slowly... it nodded.

The square went completely quiet. And then everybody started cheering. For me. The mayor even gave me a medal. Smaller than Xela's. But still.

That night, Xela was standing in the corner of my room again. And for the first time, I knew exactly what to say.

I smiled and said, "Xela... I hope we're NEVER friends." Xela walked across the room... and gave me a high five. Best friends. Opposite style.

We sat on my bed and I told it about my whole crazy week, like I always used to. Except this time, I knew it was listening. And for the first time, Xela didn't walk away.

I was almost asleep when I heard a whisper, coming from the corner of the room. "Goodnight, Alex... I hope you never find out which one of us is the real one."

My eyes snapped open. Because I suddenly remembered something.

Every single morning, for as long as I can remember, Xela says the same thing. "Stay in bed, Alex." And every single morning... I get up.
